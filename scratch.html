What Makes Verbier Special?

Nestled at 1,500 metres in the heart of the Swiss Alps, Verbier is the crown jewel of the 4 Vallées ski domain — the largest lift-linked ski area in Switzerland. Its north-facing slopes hold some of Europe's most reliable powder, while its legendary off-piste terrain has earned it an international reputation among elite skiers and snowboarders alike.

The resort plays host to the annual Xtreme Verbier, one of freeride skiing's most prestigious competitions, where athletes descend the near-vertical north face of the Bec des Rosses. Whether you're a nervous beginner or a seasoned expert, Verbier has a mountain for you.

The Must-Ski Runs

Verbier caters to every level. Below is a guide to the mountain's iconic runs, categorized by difficulty.

  1. Bec des Rosses (Off-Piste Expert) — The crown jewel. Site of Xtreme Verbier.
    • Gradient: up to 55 degrees
    • Requires guide and avalanche equipment
    • Not suitable for anyone below expert level
  2. Mont-Fort Couloirs (Black) — Steep, icy, and exhilarating from the highest lift.
    • Best skied early morning for hard pack
    • Watch for exposed rock outcrops in low snow years
    • Served by the Mont-Fort cable car (3,330m)
  3. Lac des Vaux (Red) — A flowing, wide cruiser at altitude.
    • Great visibility due to open terrain
    • Perfect for intermediate speed runs
    • Links to the 4 Vallées circuit towards Nendaz
  4. Savoleyres (Blue) — The quieter, family-friendly sector.
    • Separate lift system from the main Verbier area
    • Excellent for beginners and progression
    • Beautiful views towards the Grand Combin
  5. La Chaux (Green) — Ideal wide beginner runs with gentle gradients.
    • Dedicated beginner-only ski school area
    • Covered by snowmaking for reliable conditions
    • Direct access from the main village gondola
